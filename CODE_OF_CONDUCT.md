# Code of Conduct

SwiftDelta's project spaces are for respectful, focused collaboration on the software and its documentation.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- discuss ideas and code without attacking people;
- give specific, actionable technical feedback;
- welcome different backgrounds and levels of experience;
- respect privacy and confidentiality;
- acknowledge mistakes and correct them;
- stop behavior that a participant reasonably identifies as harmful.

## Unacceptable conduct

The project does not accept harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized conduct, deliberate intimidation, sustained disruption, publication of another person's private information, or retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.

Disagreement is expected. Repetition, volume, or status does not excuse hostile behavior.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, review discussions, issue discussions, project events, and private communication undertaken on behalf of SwiftDelta. It also applies when someone publicly represents the project.

## Maintainer response

Maintainers may ask a participant to change behavior, remove content, pause participation, or leave a project space. Responses should consider context, severity, prior behavior, and the safety of affected people. Confidential information should be shared only with people needed to address the report.

## Reporting conduct concerns

The source tree does not currently publish a dedicated private conduct-reporting contact. Do not submit sensitive personal details through a public channel.

For a non-confidential concern, use the moderation or issue route attached to the distribution you received. For a confidential concern, request a private route from the project owner without including the sensitive details. A direct contact should be added before the project invites confidential reports through this document.

When reporting, describe the behavior, location, approximate time, impact, and any immediate safety concern. Do not collect or distribute unrelated personal information.

## Good-faith reports

Reports made in good faith should not result in retaliation. Deliberately false reports, misuse of confidential material, and attempts to weaponize this process may themselves violate this policy.
